How to play Atemporal Strategy

Plan robot orders at a moment in the timeline, then commit. When everyone commits, the battlefield runs again from the earliest change. Inspect the new future and plan the next round. Passing is a valid turn. While waiting for another player, use Uncommit to restore your moves and edit them. The turn starts immediately when everyone commits.

Economy · Orders and groups · Survival · Controls · Graphs and replay

Build an economy

You begin with a miner, constructor, and turret. Send the miner onto ore, place a factory blueprint, and assign the constructor to build in that area. Constructors can also mine at half the miner’s throughput. Matter flows into your bank and pays for construction and production continuously. High priority gets funded before medium and low; consumers within a tier share the available matter. Off stops spending. With default content, turrets automatically repair at 0.5 HP per tick for 1 matter per HP, half their construction efficiency; repair continues while firing.

Choose whether new queue items should loop, then queue units at a factory (Shift-click adds five), and give its output a stored order so newborn units leave the output tile. Each queued item keeps its own loop flag and rejoins the tail after spawning if marked ↻. Use its ↻ / 1× button to change that item; the Loop new items switch only affects later additions. Fully paid units wait when output is blocked. Canceling paid work loses its investment. A damaged construction site keeps its damage as it grows.

Orders and control groups

Attack-move advances toward a destination and attacks when possible. Support follows an ally and assists with the unit’s available capabilities. Mining and construction use selected areas. Walkers can move diagonally without cutting wall corners; vehicles move along the axes. Allied mobile bots can yield by stepping aside or swapping when there is a legal space. Weapons, vision, movement, and work rates differ by type.

Groups use keys 0–9. A group order commands its members once and is saved for newborn units from bound factories. Individual orders can override it until another group order arrives. Factory output binding and selecting a factory as a group member are separate settings.

Rewrite the future

Stage orders at one chosen tick and undo changes until committing. When assigning an action, keep future orders, drop all later orders for the selected recipients, or use the configured following time window. Priority, queue, and other settings keep future orders. Review the estimated skipped orders. A successful replacement locks older future assignments and settings for its following interval; same-round and newer orders still apply. If its target disappears after a rewrite, no lock is installed. Group locks also protect saved orders and are inherited by newborns. Single-order mode allows one command per turn; timestamp mode permits multiple commands at the selected tick.

Survival and winning

A player needs both a completed active building (turret or factory) and a completed constructor or factory. Losing either requirement eliminates the player temporarily. Their remaining units keep acting, and finishing a factory can restore them. The simulation continues until inactivity or its maximum tick limit, including when everyone is temporarily eliminated.

The final state determines the result: everyone alive is a stalemate, some alive is a win, and nobody alive is a draw. Stalemates never score. Wins score for final survivors; teams earn their surviving-member count. Draws award nobody or everyone according to setup.

Scoreboard mode awards each resolved round, including unchanged winning passes. The default target is 5, with tied leaders continuing until one qualifies alone. Setup can change the target, use a required lead, allow shared victory, or enable thinking-time penalties. Scoreboard rounds have no automatic round limit; the host can stop and archive an unfinished match without adding points. Timed mode locks a configured span of history after each planning round. A player loses timed eligibility when that locked state contains no completed constructor or factory; losing only active buildings is not enough. Hybrid mode combines the scoreboard target with a fixed advance of immutable history each round, without timed-mode permanent elimination. Inspect the lobby’s rules for the selected match.

While the simulation runs, you can play or scrub the completed part of the new replay. The turquoise frontier marks the available ticks; the dark remainder is still being computed. Playback waits at that frontier and resumes as more ticks arrive. The result stays pending and orders remain closed until the full replay is verified. Refreshing rejoins the available preview, and final publication preserves the tick you are inspecting.

Browser controls

Choose a tick, select your units with a click or box drag, then choose an action below or press its key. Shift-click toggles a unit; Shift-drag adds units. The first command pins the draft tick. Use T to return to it, or Rebase draft here to move the draft to the inspected tick. Rebase is an estimate, and the server checks the full draft when you commit. Undo and redo preserve the command’s timestamp.

You may inspect history while planning; staging waits for exact state at the chosen tick.

Graphs, rounds and reconnecting

Statistics plots bank, mined matter, total spend, living army and infrastructure value, and spend ratio against the selected opponent. Choose players and a full-run or visible-timeline window; hover for sampled values. Thinking-time ratio uses cumulative committed durations by round, not battlefield ticks; a zero opponent duration has no defined ratio. The top bar separates committed time from the live planning timer. Cosmetic combat effects are bounded at fast playback rates.

Open “Rounds, inputs and rewrite results” to inspect a previous round. Historical rounds are read-only, even if your current draft exists. Return to live to continue planning. Accepted inputs link to their ticks and show the server’s applied counts and skipped reasons. The before/after summary compares outcomes, survivors, spending, entity births (including construction sites), and destruction or cancellation. Survival history records loss reasons and recovery separately from finalized timed losses.

A connection interruption preserves your view and draft while reconnecting to the same server. If the server instance has changed, use Reload current server for its current match, roster and guide. Team members share a map color; owner numbers preserve individual identity.

Unbuilt blueprints are selectable: configure their unit queue, priority, looping, and starting order before construction. Production begins when construction completes. The matter counter follows the viewed tick. Fog of war uses your units’ vision (shared with allies); spectators see the whole battlefield. The header scoreboard shows current match totals: completed timelines won by each player, the win target, and who is leading. These totals stay current while you inspect older ticks or rounds; a running simulation only scores when its result is published. Team or adjusted points appear separately when they differ from individual wins. Purple timeline markers show every applied command tick for the selection. Green WIN and red LOSS describe the timeline’s final survival result; elimination can reverse during simulation, and match completion is labeled separately.

Units start without a control group. Group numbers appear only after assignment; queued membership and priority changes appear immediately at the draft tick, including a Mixed label when the selection differs. Use the shared Matter priority dropdown for constructors, factories, turrets and unfinished buildings. Priority appears only in the bottom UI. Construction funding uses the target building’s priority; a constructor set to Off contributes no spending. Ore is yellow until exhausted, when it becomes gray; walls are darkest gray, unseen floor is medium gray, and seen floor is lightest.

In Statistics, choose Display → Slope (smoothed) to compare rates such as matter mined per tick. The slope uses a local linear fit over up to five adjacent samples, does not bridge missing data, and can be negative. Thinking-time slopes use rounds instead of ticks.
